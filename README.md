# EU AI Act: human-centred governance and accessibility

**Double FW Ltd · Version 0.1.0 · 11 November 2026 · MIT licence**

Understand the EU AI Act, organise its delivery and evaluate what AI means for the people who use, operate or are affected by it. This knowledge base connects legal scope, organisational policy, day-to-day workflows, accessibility, evidence and reporting.

It covers AI in **websites, web apps, native mobile and desktop apps, APIs, back-office systems, workplace tools, voice interfaces, agents, media production, general-purpose models and regulated products**. A website or app is not automatically an AI system. Duties depend on the AI use, operator, EU connection, classification, applicable date and any specific exclusion or sectoral rule.

## First six actions

1. Choose one actual AI use and identify its human service owner.
2. Complete the [system and scope record](templates/system-and-scope-record.md), including the people affected and all connected surfaces.
3. Follow workflows WF01–WF03 in the [runbook](docs/governance-runbook.md) to verify sources, decide applicability and map duties.
4. Plan supplier, risk, data, accessibility and oversight work. Use the [assessment plan](templates/assessment-and-test-record.md).
5. Gather actual evidence, correct material problems and prepare the appropriate decision and reporting records.
6. Maintain the system through changes, complaints, monitoring, incident response and retirement.

An urgent access barrier can be addressed immediately through the service owner's support process while the wider assessment continues.

## How to read requirements

**Law** means a sourced legal requirement with its role, scope, conditions and application date. **Interpretation** means a reasoned reading requiring the relevant facts. **Recommended practice** means a Double-FW delivery approach that an organisation can adopt. **Evidence** means an observation or authenticated decision from the actual system and context.

Published guidance, a supplier assertion, a passing scan and a completed template answer different questions. None alone establishes complete AI Act compliance. Keep technical accessibility, output quality, fair treatment, human control and remedy as separate findings.

The legal reference date is **11 November 2026**, using the AI Act as amended in July 2026. Check [status](sources/legal-and-standards-status.md) before a live classification, legal claim or standards decision. National and sector rules need local verification.

This is an initial knowledge release and proposed organisational operating method. The package contains blank templates and explicitly illustrative scenarios, with no real participant data or service approvals.
