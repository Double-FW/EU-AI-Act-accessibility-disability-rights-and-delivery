## RP01 — Identify the audience and legal purpose

There is no single universal EU AI Act accessibility report. Separate management reporting, public service information, individual explanations, conformity documents, FRIA notification, registration, incident reports and authority responses. EAA/WAD/AVMSD/privacy/sector formats and recipients can differ.

For each output record entity/role, system/use, jurisdiction, exact provision, audience, responsible/authorised owner, format/language/access needs, trigger, due-time calculation, source evidence, version, approval and actual submission/publication evidence. Use the current authority's instructions for a statutory handoff. [L01 Articles 16–27, 43–55, 72–94; other laws as applicable.]

## RP02 — Management reporting

Recommended governance reports should make decisions possible rather than count documents. Include:

1. Scope: systems/uses and people covered, countries, surfaces, period and changes.
2. Obligations: current versus future duties, unverified classification and material source changes.
3. Performance: separate access, output quality, fair treatment, control and remedy evidence.
4. Risks/issues: severity, actual/possible harm, unresolved evidence, owner, correction and retest.
5. Capability: supplier cooperation, oversight/literacy support, staffing and resources.
6. Actions: decision needed, accountable function, deadline/rationale and escalation.

Keep a lack of evidence visible. Do not average away critical barriers or omit people who could not complete a task. State sample size, test environment, variability and untested scope. Local monthly/quarterly reporting is recommended planning, not a statutory cadence.

## RP03 — Keep formal outputs separate

| Output | Typical legal route | Evidence and limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| EU declaration and CE marking | Applicable high-risk provider route, Articles 43–48. | Actual assessed system and applicable laws; internal versus notified-body route verified. |
| EU database registration | Article 49 and relevant Article 26 provisions. | Correct system/entity/use and route; restricted/national exceptions considered. |
| FRIA results notification | Article 27. | Qualifying deployer/use, completed assessment and required current template/route, with real sending evidence. |
| GPAI public training-content summary | Article 53. | Required template/content and copyright policy obligations; not a full training-data publication claim. |
| Individual explanation | Conditional Article 86 and other applicable rights. | AI role and main decision elements within the right's scope, accessible response and case evidence. |
| AI notice or content disclosure | Article 50. | Applicable paragraph/actor/exception, first interaction/exposure and accessibility; machine marking separately verified. |
| Service accessibility information/statement | EAA Annex V/national rules or WAD model, where applicable. | Correct legal format and scope; neither substitutes for AI conformity evidence. |
| Media accessibility progress report | AVMSD/national regime. | Applicable reporting period, service/route and actual usable provision; no invented EU quota. |
| Authority request/cooperation | Relevant AI/sector/privacy provisions. | Correct authority, requested scope, protected information and actual response. |

A VPAT/accessibility conformance report can organise relevant procurement evidence. It is not a universal EU AI Act report, a FRIA, an EU declaration or proof that every statement is verified.

## RP04 — Route incidents and calculate the clock

An accessibility defect is not automatically an Article 73 serious incident. Conversely, serious fundamental-rights harm can be relevant without physical injury. Investigate the definition, actor and applicable duties promptly. One event can also trigger privacy/security, product, EAA, equality or sector processes.

| Route | Trigger and timing to verify | Operational response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Article 26(5), deployer risk | Reason to consider relevant use may present the stated risk. Inform relevant parties/authority without undue delay and suspend as required by the provision. | Protect people, record risk and notify using the actual route. |
| Article 26(5), deployer serious incident | Identified serious incident; immediately inform first provider, then relevant importer/distributor and authorities as specified. If provider cannot be reached, check Article 73 application. | Preserve sequence and timestamps; do not await a final investigation. |
| Article 73(2), provider | Immediately after establishing causal link or reasonable likelihood, and no later than 15 days after relevant awareness. | Calculate from real chronology; earlier action may be required. |
| Article 73(3), specified cases | Widespread infringement or the specified critical-infrastructure serious incident: immediately and no later than two days after awareness. | Escalate promptly with qualifying facts. |
| Article 73(4), death | Immediately after establishing or suspecting the causal relationship, no later than ten days after awareness. | Retain both awareness and suspicion/causation timestamps. |
| Article 73(5) and investigation | An incomplete initial report can be followed by a complete report where necessary for timely reporting. Investigation/cooperation and evidence-preservation conditions apply. | Record initial/follow-up sequence; avoid changing evidence before required authority information. |
| GPAI systemic-risk incident | Article 55(1)(c) and applicable AI Office route. | Do not substitute the provider high-risk 15-day rule automatically. |
| Other regimes | Actual GDPR/security, product, accessibility and national/sector thresholds and clocks. | Parallel routes, recipients and owners recorded independently. |

These are summaries of L01; sector-specific reporting adjustments and competent authority routes require verification. The time limits are outer bounds, not a permission to wait. Confirm time zone, day-counting rules and current submission mechanism. If facts are incomplete, preserve uncertainty and follow applicable initial reporting duties.

## RP05 — Records, retention and privacy

Keep required logs and documentation under their actual provisions. For example, provider documentation under Article 18 generally has a ten-year requirement; deployer-controlled logs under Article 26(6) generally have an at-least-six-month requirement subject to applicable law. Do not apply one number to all customer conversations or research recordings.

Set a record-specific retention schedule considering data protection, legal obligations, sector rules, purpose, access and deletion. Keep personal data and sensitive evidence in authorised protected systems. Reports should use minimised/aggregated information without making small groups identifiable. Record access controls and protected evidence references rather than publish the evidence itself.

## RP06 — Control public claims and accessible communication

Every claim needs a scope, version, date, evidence and accountable publication decision. Say what was assessed and what remains untested. Do not claim universal accessibility, “AI Act certified” status or legal compliance solely from adopting this repository, passing a scan or purchasing a model.

Use structured headings, meaningful links, accessible tables/documents, clear language and appropriate alternate formats. Check mobile/AT access and actual recipient comprehension where important. Preserve necessary legal qualifications when simplifying information. D07, D09 and D14 support communication design; they do not approve the legal claim.

Draft, approved, sent, received, published, corrected and withdrawn are distinct states. Retain verified receipts/locations when available. This repository prepares records; it does not submit to authorities or publish automatically.
