## LI01 — One service can have several legal profiles

The AI Act supplements other law. Keep each instrument's entity, scope, country, requirements, dates, exceptions, authority and reporting route separately traceable. Shared evidence can support several conclusions; an exemption or pass under one does not automatically transfer.

| Instrument / source | Connection with AI delivery | What to organise | Avoid this inference |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| EAA, L05 | Covered products and consumer services can use AI; Article 16(l) also expressly references its accessibility requirements for high-risk providers. | Applicable Annex I functions, accessible service information, support, sector functions, conformity/corrective processes and national rules. | Every website or all workplace software is independently covered by EAA. |
| WAD, L06 | Covered public-sector websites/apps can present or use AI. | Exact scope/exclusions, applicable EN mapping, statement, feedback, monitoring and enforcement. | Every publicly funded entity is covered; public-service broadcasters have a specific exclusion. |
| AVMSD, L07 | AI can create content and access features used in media services. | Article 7 progressive accessibility, reporting, accessible emergency information, national/licence rules and actual delivery quality. | A single universal EU caption quota exists, or an AI-generated access file establishes access. |
| Employment equality, L09 | AI can affect recruitment, work, assessment and progression. | Job-related validity, discrimination review, reasonable accommodation and individual remedies. | An accessible form establishes a fair ranking model, or a private employer always needs an Article 27 FRIA. |
| GDPR, L08 | AI processes personal data, inferences and potentially special-category data. | Lawful basis/conditions, minimisation, transparency, security, data rights, DPIA and Article 22 where applicable. | A human signature is always meaningful involvement; research consent permits every downstream model use. |
| Procurement, L10 | Public procurement for public/staff use includes accessibility specification duties, subject to scope/conditions. | Requirements, equivalent evidence, configuration/integration tests and supplier cooperation. | A supplier questionnaire, VPAT or CE mark answers every procurement or AI duty. |
| Communications, L11 and EAA | Voice, text, video and emergency services can involve AI. | Equivalent access/choice, relevant real-time text/total conversation/emergency arrangements and independent service rules. | An AI voice assistant automatically replaces a required accessible communication mode. |
| Medical and other Annex I law, L12/L01 | Some AI is a regulated product/safety component. | Intended purpose, product qualification, third-party-assessment condition, Annex I section and sector integration. | Any assistive app is a medical device, or all Annex I products follow identical AI Act duties. |
| UNCRPD and Charter, L13–L14 | Dignity, equality, privacy, access and participation inform AI Act protections, including Recital 80. | Rights-based questions and actual enforceable implementing routes; Charter Article 51 scope. | A treaty article or recital is a standalone product certificate or universally identical private-law claim. |
| DSA, L15 | Relevant platforms/recommenders and very large services have separate duties. | Applicable transparency/risk/choice rules and special-category advertising restrictions. | Recommendations described as minimal AI risk have no other obligations. |
| Consumer protection, L16 | Manipulative, misleading or aggressive AI-enabled sales practices can harm people. | Foreseeable vulnerability in context, truthful claims, choices and cancellation. | Failure to meet AI Act Article 5's harm test establishes consumer-law compliance. |
| Accessible-format copyright, L17 | AI can assist permitted reading-format transformations. | Beneficiary, work, lawful access, authorised use/entity and exchange conditions. | Accessibility purpose supplies blanket permission for commercial model training or unrestricted uploads. |

## LI02 — EAA customer outcomes and functional coverage

Annex I addresses accessible information and interfaces, support, interoperability and sector functions. Banking includes accessible identification/security/payment and its B2 information-complexity condition. Covered media-access services include programme guides and transmission/preservation of access features. Exact product/service categories and national implementation matter.

Section VII's functional criteria cover sensory, speech, physical, cognitive, seizure and privacy needs. They address functions not otherwise covered and tightly qualified alternative approaches. Use them to ask whether the person can achieve the function; do not substitute them indiscriminately for technical requirements or treat them as diagnoses.

EAA microenterprise service exemptions, disproportionate burden, fundamental alteration and legacy content/product transitions have specific conditions. They do not automatically exempt an AI provider from Article 16(l), the AI Act generally or equality law. A legal owner should map overlapping scope precisely.

## LI03 — Media requires a delivery chain

Distinguish AVMSD media-service accessibility from EAA's covered services providing access to audiovisual media. Track the exact programme edition, access asset, master, package/transcode, transmitted route, player and audience outcome. Review captions, description and signing with appropriate language/editorial expertise and intended users.

Separately decide whether synthetic content is a deepfake or otherwise engages Article 50. Creative-work qualifications do not mean every fiction programme is exempt from all disclosure; an artificial access-service voice is not automatically a deepfake. A visual disclosure can itself be inaccessible.

## LI04 — Employment, decisions and accommodations

A neutral-looking timed task, speech measure, facial inference or historic employment pattern can raise validity, disability and discrimination questions. Investigate the actual system and job. Preserve individual adjustment routes and equivalent opportunities rather than force everyone through an AI channel.

Consider the interaction between classification, prohibited emotion inference, data protection, worker information, accommodation and explanation/complaint rights. Do not infer diagnosis, capacity or ability from typing speed or assistive-technology use. D08, D10 and D11 provide recommended methods.

## LI05 — National and UK adapters

For each country add the actual implementing law, competent authority, sector scope, reporting format, language and any stricter requirements. This repository has not selected or approved national adapters.

A UK organisation can fall within EU territorial rules. Great Britain equality/service/employment duties, UK public-sector accessibility rules and Northern Ireland's separate framework require their own applicability records. EU obligations do not replace domestic ones. S08 is an official starting point, not a complete UK legal analysis.
