# Contributing

Contributions should make the knowledge clearer, more useful and better supported. Use British English, descriptive headings, meaningful links, simple tables and explanations for abbreviations. Preserve stable IDs; deprecate rather than silently reuse an ID for a different meaning.

## Propose a change

1. Identify the user task, barrier, source change or incorrect claim.
2. Read the owning document and relevant legal/status/source records. Follow `AGENTS.md` for AI-assisted work.
3. Change the owning guidance, then dependent maps, templates and status where needed.
4. Explain the evidence, source/date, applicability, changed behaviour and remaining limits.
5. Check local links, identifiers, heading structure, source references, legal/standards consistency and rights. Use the validation record to state what was actually checked.

Legal wording needs an exact primary provision and its conditions. Method recommendations need a rationale and appropriate evidence. Do not turn a preferred technique, sample size, local score or automated rule into an invented legal requirement.

## Evidence and privacy

Use fictional or approved de-identified examples only, clearly labelled. Never publish credentials, confidential cases, personal data, participant recordings or private source snapshots. Do not submit generated participant testimony or fabricated tool results as real evidence.

For real methods, document scope, versions, procedure, result, negative evidence and limitations. Accessibility and disability are diverse; avoid diagnostic inference and blanket assumptions.

## Rights and review

Contribute only material you have authority to contribute under this repository's MIT licence. Preserve source attribution and external rights. Link to standards and specialist guidance rather than copy substantial copyrighted material.

Maintainers and appropriately qualified reviewers should examine changes affecting legal interpretations, safety, privacy, equality or material compliance claims. Routine editorial changes can proceed within the contributor's existing mandate. Approval of a repository change does not approve a real service or legal conclusion.
