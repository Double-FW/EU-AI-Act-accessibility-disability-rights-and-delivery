# Worked routing scenarios

**Illustrative facts only · No real system was classified, tested or approved.**

These examples demonstrate reasoning and evidence needs. All test results remain **Not tested**. Actual legal conclusions require complete facts and current sources.

## EX01 — Retail chatbot on a website and native app

**Assumed facts:** A retailer uses a supplier chatbot for delivery information on its website and native app. It does not evaluate credit or eligibility. The model and interface are separate supplier layers.

**Routing:** WF02 assesses actual provider/deployer roles, territorial scope and Article 50. Do not classify high-risk merely because the tool is a chatbot. A covered e-commerce service has its own EAA obligations. If the tool later evaluates creditworthiness, reopen scope rather than reuse the support-only decision.

**Delivery:** D07 supports conversation states, notices, history, streaming, recovery and handoff. D03 supports technical assessment. The native app needs platform-specific tests in addition to the web plan. D09 supports understandable information and return after interruption.

**Evidence to obtain:** actual first-interaction notice, accessible host/authentication, configured SDK behaviour, native AT/navigation, real generated answers, failed connection, human-support availability and relevant marking/exception facts. Use AI-A01–AI-A08 and AI-A16/18 as applicable. A supplier demo is not the deployed-service evidence.

## EX02 — Recruitment screening behind an accessible portal

**Assumed facts:** An employer uses a supplier's model to rank candidates. Applications arrive through web and mobile interfaces, with a recruiter dashboard and a separate accommodation process.

**Routing:** Examine the exact Annex III employment point and all conditions; human final selection does not automatically remove high-risk classification. Investigate whether any biometric/emotion functionality engages Article 5. Record employer/supplier roles and worker/applicant information. An ordinary private employer is not automatically subject to Article 27 FRIA solely because recruitment AI is high-risk; DPIA/equality and other duties require their own analysis.

**Delivery:** D10 supplies recruitment/accommodation and fair-review methods; D08 data/participation ethics; D11 cumulative task burden; D03 interface evidence. Evaluate validity and proxies separately from portal accessibility.

**Evidence to obtain:** job relevance, data/proxy assumptions, performance/context gaps, accommodation effectiveness, actual reviewer intervention, accessible adverse-decision communication and challenge route. Use AI-A09–AI-A11 and relevant interface tests. A technical portal pass cannot establish lawful rankings.

## EX03 — AI in a physical assistive product

**Assumed facts:** A supplier proposes an AI-enabled device intended to assist mobility. Its exact medical purpose and safety classification are initially unknown.

**Routing:** The intended purpose and applicable product law must be established before the Article 6(1) route is decided. Identify Annex I section and third-party-assessment conditions. If Section B applies, use Article 2(2)'s tailored route; do not impose all ordinary high-risk duties automatically. An assistive marketing claim alone establishes neither a medical-device classification nor exemption.

**Delivery:** Add physical controls, reach/strength, sensory alerts, instructions, app and operator control to the scope. Use D04's scope/evidence approach and qualified product/safety/accessibility methods. AI-A12 must use the approved safe protocol, not an improvised hazardous failure.

**Evidence to obtain:** qualification, exact standards/sector rules, safe intervention, real-device access, alternate communication and expert/participant observations. Missing product facts keep classification Undetermined while independent documentation/design work continues.

## EX04 — AI-generated access features and synthetic media

**Assumed facts:** A media service uses speech recognition for captions, AI to draft description, and generated footage in a programme.

**Routing:** Separately evaluate tool/system scope, Article 50 provider/deployer duties, whether footage meets the deepfake definition, any creative-work qualification and the media service's national AVMSD/EAA duties. Do not assume every access-service voice is a deepfake or every edited sentence needs the same label.

**Delivery:** D06 manages exact programme/asset/route evidence. D12/D13/D18 support editorial review, commissioning and delivered access. D07 is relevant only if a conversational UI is also present.

**Evidence to obtain:** meaning, names, speaker/non-speech information, timing, description fidelity and delivery through the actual package/player. Check accessible human disclosure and relevant machine marking separately. Use AI-A13/14. A correctly formatted caption file can still be inaccurate or absent from the viewer's route.

## EX05 — Customer-chosen support through an agent

**Assumed facts:** A customer asks an agent to find an appropriate service and submit a booking. They choose text and short step-by-step instructions. The service records aggregate satisfaction about navigation and completion.

**Routing:** Classify the actual use, tools/actions, roles and EU connection; assisting a disabled customer is not itself an exemption or high-risk category. Assess Article 50 and other service/data duties. Do not infer medical status from preferences.

**Delivery:** D01/D08/D09 support delegation, minimum data, continuity and choice. Review tool permissions, consequential confirmation, cancellation, records and recovery. Aggregated findings may inform an improvement agent only within lawful processing and real delegation; small cells may still identify people.

**Evidence to obtain:** accessible preference/authorisation, scoped tool actions, resistance to untrusted content, no unintended booking duplication, revocation and usable history. Use AI-A05/06/15/16. Generated personas cannot replace customer research or establish universal satisfaction.
